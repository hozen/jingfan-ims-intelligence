## 3. Order Intake / Booking Data for Capital Equipment

**Important finding:** Danaher does **not** separately disclose "order intake" or "bookings" for capital equipment vs. consumables in its 10-K filings. The company uses the term "Remaining Performance Obligations" (RPO) rather than "backlog" or "order intake."

### Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)

Danaher discloses RPO figures which serve as a proxy for backlog:

| Year | RPO | % Expected in Next 12 Months |
|------|-----|----------------------------|
| **FY2025** | ~$5.2 billion | ~47% |
| **FY2024** | ~$4.3 billion | ~47% |
| **FY2023** | Not explicitly stated in 10-K | — |

> **FY2025 Quote:** "As of December 31, 2025, the aggregate amount of the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations was approximately $ 5.2 billion. The Company expects to recognize revenue on approximately 47 % of the remaining performance obligations over the next 12 months, 26 % over the next 13-24 months."

> **FY2024 Quote:** "As of December 31, 2024, the aggregate amount of the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations was approximately $ 4.3 billion. The Company expects to recognize revenue on approximately 47 % of the remaining performance obligations over the next 12 months, 27 % over the next 13-24 months."

### RPO vs. Traditional Backlog

> **Quote:** "While the remaining performance obligation disclosure is similar in concept to backlog, the definition of remaining performance obligations excludes leases and contracts that provide the customer with the right to cancel or terminate for convenience with no substantial penalty, even if historical experience indicates the likelihood of cancellation or termination is remote. Additionally, the Company has elected to exclude contracts with customers with an original term of one year or less from remaining performance obligations while these contracts are included within backlog."

**Key insight:** Danaher explicitly links RPO to backlog, noting RPO is similar to backlog but excludes:
- Short-term contracts (≤1 year)
- Cancelable contracts
- Equipment leases

## 4. Backlog Figures

Danaher does not publish a specific "backlog" figure separate from RPO. The 10-Ks use RPO as the primary metric for unfulfilled orders.

The company notes that backlog includes:
- Equipment leased to customers
- Contracts with original term >1 year

RPO excludes these items but provides a comparable metric.

**No specific dollar backlog figure is disclosed** in the 10-Ks for the periods covered.

## 6. Capital Equipment vs. Consumables — Revenue Recognition

Danaher's 10-Ks do **not** separately disclose revenue for capital equipment vs. consumables by segment. However, the MD&A provides context:

> **Biotechnology Segment:** "The Biotechnology segment includes the bioprocessing and discovery and medical businesses and offers a broad range of equipment, consumables and services."

The company notes in the RPO discussion that:
- Consumable supply arrangements are **excluded** from RPO
- Equipment leased to customers is **excluded** from RPO
- Extended warranty and service contracts are **included**

This implies:
- **Equipment/Instrument revenue** is captured in RPO (with leased equipment excluded)
- **Consumables** are NOT in RPO (recognized upon delivery)
- **Service/Warranty** is included in RPO

### Order-to-Ship Cycle Context

The company does not explicitly disclose order-to-ship cycles in the 10-K. However, the RPO disclosure of ~47% recognition within 12 months suggests:
- A significant portion of equipment orders ship within 12 months
- The remaining 53% represents longer-cycle equipment (potentially 13-36 months)
